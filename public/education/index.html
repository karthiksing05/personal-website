education

learning by building, and building to learn

What I cannot create, I do not understand. — Richard Feynman, American theoretical physicist

Most of what I know about my own research I did not learn from reading the paper - I learned it from watching the algorithm do something I did not expect, and then working backwards to figure out why. Indeed, a figure in a paper is a single frozen moment of a process, and it is in interacting with that process (changing an input, stepping through a decision, breaking an assumption on purpose) that a reader builds the intuition the paper was trying to transfer in the first place. This page collects interactive explainers for the concepts behind my research, each one a small simulation that lets you poke at an idea until it stops being abstract. Fundamentally, I believe that good visualization is a form of argument, and that the best way to understand a theory of learning is to watch it learn.

Each card opens its own simulation, written in the register of the papers it draws from and linked to the original work - please reach out if something is unclear or if you would like to see a concept covered!

Cobweb: Concept Formation as Information Gain

An interactive walkthrough of Fisher’s (1987) Cobweb as recent work implements it: an incremental learner that sorts each experience into a taxonomy of probabilistic concepts, choosing among four restructuring operators to maximize the information its concepts carry about their members. The simulation steps through each operator, traces a prediction through the hierarchy, revisits three classic categorization experiments, and closes on the applications of Cobweb to semantic retrieval and lifelong topic modeling.
