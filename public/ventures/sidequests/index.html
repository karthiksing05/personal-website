SideQuests

Turn waiting into wandering.

Tell it where you are, when you need to be back and what you're in the mood for. You get three timed plans of nearby events and places, with the travel between them, in under a second. Rate what you did and the next plan fits better. Open a plan and people nearby can join.

Three of us met on a study-abroad program this summer, some of us first in Berlin. We kept running into the same problem: an open afternoon, a layover or a delayed train, and no fast way to turn it into something worth doing.

Tech stack

One VPS, four services

The phone shows things, Go owns the rules, and the ML service does the scoring. Tap a box for details, or trace one plan request.

A plan request touches the app, nginx, Go, MongoDB and the ML service, in about half a second.

AI stack

Rank what you'll like, then plan what fits

A small trained ranker scores stops against your taste and today's mood. A graph search turns the best of them into three plans you can actually do.

Eight-section textsLikes, dislikes, mood and every event, written the same way. Facebook likes via Meta's Graph API give a head start.
Qwen3 embedding0.6B, frozen, 1024-d. Cache → Vertex AI → Hugging Face → local, parity-gated.
VibeMatch1.85M params, 7.4 MB. Reads likes and dislikes. 200 pairs in ~6 ms on CPU.
Questline plannerK-best search over a time graph: 3 varied plans in 0.2–0.6 s.
Jev rerankAn LLM judge re-scores the top 20 after the reply, so it never slows you down.

Training with no users

  1. Qwen3.5-9B on my academic compute wrote 100k event listings and 10k personas.
  2. Each user got 20 candidates, including trick events that look right but hit a dislike.
  3. An LLM judge rated 200k pairs from 0 to 3.
  4. 21 runs on A100s took 12 minutes; the winner was picked on validation.
  5. After launch, every rating moves your taste live: likes ← 0.8·likes + 0.2·activity. No retraining.

Data agents keep the catalog fresh: Meta's Muse Spark researches each new event with cited sources, and Gemini writes its profile.

Watch the planner build three paths

Tech Square, 2:10 to 6:30 PM. Each stop at a start time is a node; an edge exists only if you can get there in time, so edges only point forward. Dashed boxes are candidates the search passed over.

for v in sort_by_start(stops):
    for u in preds(v):
        for path in best[u]:
            if allowed(path, v):
                best[v].add(path + v)
    best[v] = top_k(best[v])      # k = 16
value(v)   = (score(v) − τ) / (1 − τ)
cost(u, v) = β·travel + λ·idle
plans = pick_diverse(top_paths)

Every plan is

  • inside your window, with travel and a 10-minute buffer
  • within budget and range; unknown prices never count as free
  • age-appropriate and true to your mood ("no bars")
  • free of repeat venues and categories
  • repaired up to 3 rounds if it has gaps or weak stops

Things to try

  1. Tap +, set your window and vibe, and get three options.
  2. Change the back-by time to 5:30 PM and see the plans shrink to fit.
  3. Move a stop up or down and watch the transit re-time, and turn red if you'd be late.
  4. Start the plan, then find it on Home.
  5. On the Forum, post that you're free or join Maya's open plan.
  6. In Groups, send a message or add an expense and check the split.
  7. Rate a past outing and watch your taste profile move in Account.

Stops, times and people are illustrative. Travel times use a simple distance model, like the app's fallback.
